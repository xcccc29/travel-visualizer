Day-by-Day Plan

Day 1 Salzburg

Day 1 — May 21 | Arrive Munich → Salzburg

Transportation: - S-Bahn S8/S1 from Munich Airport to Munich Hauptbahnhof (~40 min) - ÖBB Railjet from Munich Hbf to Salzburg Hbf (~1h 30min)

In Salzburg: - Check into hotel, settle in - Evening walk along the Salzach River and across the Staatsbrücke (State Bridge) into the Old Town - Race packet pickup if scheduled this evening

Stiftskeller St. Peter (St. Peter Bezirk 1/4) One of the oldest continuously operating restaurants in Europe, founded 803 AD inside a monastery courtyard. Austrian classics: Salzburger Nockerl, Tafelspitz.

Day 2 Salzburg — Pre-Race Day

Day 2 — May 22 | Salzburg — Pre-Race Day

Keep it easy on the legs today.

Morning: - Mozart’s Birthplace (Geburtshaus) — Getreidegasse 9. The house where Mozart was born in 1756, now a detailed museum - Walk through Getreidegasse, Salzburg’s medieval main street — iron guild signs, bakeries, the old city rhythm

Afternoon: - Mirabell Palace & Gardens — Baroque gardens, flat and easy on the legs - Mozart’s Residence (Mozartwohnhaus) — Makartplatz 8. His later home; more personal artifacts and letters than the birthplace - Salzburg Cathedral (Dom) — Baroque masterpiece. Mozart was baptized here and served as court organist. Sit in the acoustic for a moment.

Evening: - Optional: evening chamber concert in one of the Old Town churches (check salzburg-concerts.at for May program) - Early night — race tomorrow

Café Tomaselli (Alter Markt 9) Salzburg’s oldest coffee house, open since 1705. Light Austrian fare, excellent cakes, Melange coffee. Mozart’s family frequented it.

Day 3 Race Day

Day 3 — May 23 | Race Day

Morning: - Fuel up with a familiar breakfast — don’t experiment today - Trail Run Race — the main event

Post-Race: - Recovery walk along the Salzach riverbank, no hills - Augustinerbräu Mülln (Augustinergasse 4) — legendary self-service beer hall inside a functioning monastery brewery, open since 1621. Get a stone mug of Märzen, grab pretzels and cold cuts from the market stalls inside. Earned.

Bärenwirt (Müllner Hauptstraße 8) Unpretentious Salzburg tavern, no tourist markup. Excellent Käsespätzle (cheese noodles), Tafelspitz, local Stiegl beer on tap.

Day 4 Salzburg — Deeper Exploration

Day 4 — May 24 | Salzburg — Deeper Exploration

Morning: - Hohensalzburg Fortress — Take the funicular up; walk down through the gardens if legs allow. One of the best-preserved medieval fortresses in Central Europe. Panoramic Alpine views over the city.

Afternoon: - Untersberg Mountain — Take Bus 25 from Salzburg Hbf (~30 min) to the Untersberg Seilbahn (cable car base). Cable car rises to 1,776m (~€28 return). The ridgeline straddles the Austrian-German border. Easy walking on top, dramatic Alpine scenery. - Kapuzinerberg hill walk — across the river from the Old Town, quieter side. Stefan Zweig (Austrian author of The World of Yesterday, Chess Story) lived here 1919–1934 before fleeing the Nazis. Forested, excellent views back over the city.

Zum Wohl (Gstättengasse 3) Excellent Austrian wine bar with thoughtful seasonal small plates. Good spot to sit long and talk.

Day 5 Vienna

Day 5 — May 25 | Salzburg → Vienna

Morning: - Final walk through Old Town, Getreidegasse morning market - Check out of hotel

Transportation: - ÖBB Railjet: Salzburg Hbf → Wien Hauptbahnhof (~2h 30min) - Wien Hauptbahnhof has direct U1 U-Bahn access into the city center

Afternoon: - Check in, walk the Ringstraße — the grand 19th-century imperial boulevard: Opera House, Parliament, Kunsthistorisches Museum, Burgtheater, Rathaus in one sweep - Naschmarkt — Vienna’s famous open-air market, closes ~6pm. Cheeses, cured meats, fresh produce, spices.

Evening: - Vienna State Opera (Wiener Staatsoper) — check program at wiener-staatsoper.at. Standing tickets (Stehplatz) cost €4–13, sold in person 80 min before curtain at the Abendkasse. One of the world’s great opera houses, remarkably accessible.

Figlmüller Bäckerstraße (Bäckerstraße 6) Vienna’s most iconic Wiener Schnitzel — enormous, paper-thin, perfectly fried. Reservation recommended.

Day 6 Vienna — Imperial Collections & Music

Day 6 — May 26 | Vienna — Imperial Collections & Music

Morning: - Kunsthistorisches Museum (KHM) — Maria-Theresien-Platz. One of the world’s great art museums: the largest Bruegel collection anywhere, Vermeer, Raphael, Caravaggio, Egyptian antiquities. Allow 3+ hours.

Afternoon: - Belvedere Palace & Gardens — Upper Belvedere houses Klimt’s The Kiss and Schiele. Formal gardens are a good slow walk between the two palace buildings. - Check for afternoon concerts at the Musikverein (musikverein.at) — the Golden Hall is where the New Year’s Concert is held.

Evening: - Café Central (Herrengasse 14) — One of Vienna’s grandest coffeehouses, under soaring vaulted ceilings. Freud, Trotsky, Schnitzler, and Peter Altenberg were regulars here. Order a Melange and Apfelstrudel.

Meixner’s Gastwirtschaft (Buchfeldgasse 6) A genuine Viennese Beisl (neighborhood pub), no tourist pricing. Gulasch, liver with apples and onions, Tafelspitz.

Day 7 Vienna — Philosophy, Literature & Nature

Day 7 — May 27 | Vienna — Philosophy, Literature & Nature

Morning: - Freud Museum (Berggasse 19) — Freud’s apartment and consulting rooms, preserved as they were when he fled to London in 1938. Essential for anyone interested in the philosophy of mind and fin-de-siècle Viennese intellectual culture. Allow 1.5 hours. - Votivkirche — Neo-Gothic church on the Ringstraße, 10-minute walk from the Freud Museum.

Afternoon — choose one:

Wienerwald (Vienna Woods) Take U4 to Hütteldorf, then trails directly into the forested hills west of the city. 30 minutes from the center to proper forest trails. Beethoven walked here regularly while composing — he cited these woods as essential to his creative process.

Zentralfriedhof (Central Cemetery) Take Tram 71 from Schwarzenbergplatz. Beethoven, Schubert, Brahms, Johann Strauss father and son, and Gluck are all buried here. A quiet, serious pilgrimage through musical history. Robert Musil (The Man Without Qualities) is buried here too.

Evening: - Café Hawelka (Dorotheergasse 6) — Vienna’s beloved literary café, opened 1939. Smaller and more intimate than Café Central. Order the Buchteln (sweet rolls with jam, served warm in the evening) — a Hawelka institution.

Zum Wohl Vienna (Bauernmarkt 13) Natural wine bar with seasonal Austrian small plates. Good for a longer, relaxed shared dinner.

Day 8 Munich

Day 8 — May 28 | Vienna → Munich

Morning: - Schönbrunn Palace & Gardens — Take U4 to Schönbrunn. The formal gardens are free to walk; climb to the Gloriette hilltop for the best panoramic view over the palace and city. Allow 1.5–2 hours.

Transportation: - ÖBB/DB Railjet: Wien Hauptbahnhof → Munich Hauptbahnhof (~4 hours) - Scenic route through Upper Austria and the pre-Alpine foothills. Book in advance.

Afternoon: - Check into Munich hotel - Englischer Garten — one of the world’s largest urban parks. The Eisbach river has a standing wave where surfers ride year-round near the Prinzregentenstraße entrance. Good for a jog or walk after the train. - Viktualienmarkt browse if time allows (closes ~6pm)

Augustiner Keller (Arnulfstraße 52) Munich’s oldest beer cellar, established 1328. Massive outdoor garden under chestnut trees. Schweinsbraten (slow-roasted pork) or Obatzda (spiced cheese spread) with pretzels.

Day 9 Nördlingen + Munich Evening

Day 9 — May 29 | Nördlingen Day Trip + Munich Evening

Why Nördlingen

Nördlingen is one of the primary real-world inspirations for the walled cities in Attack on Titan. It is one of only three completely intact medieval walled cities in Germany — and uniquely, it was built inside a 25km-wide meteorite crater formed 14.5 million years ago. The crater gives the town walls their near-perfect circular shape, which is unmistakably the visual source for the walls in the anime. Hajime Isayama has confirmed the connection. Standing on the wall and looking inward at the circular town, the reference is immediately obvious.

Transportation (both ways): - Munich Hbf → Donauwörth: DB Regional Express (RE), ~55 min - Donauwörth → Nördlingen: DB Regional train (RB), ~30 min - Total one way: ~1h 30min - Buy tickets on DB Navigator app or at the station machine — no advance booking needed - Trains run roughly every hour

Suggested timing: - Depart Munich ~8:30am → Arrive Nördlingen ~10:00am - Depart Nördlingen ~14:00 → Back in Munich ~15:30

In Nördlingen (~3–4 hours):

  1. Walk the city wall circuit (2.7km) — The entire medieval wall is walkable from above, roofed like a covered gallery. Circumnavigating it takes about 45–60 minutes. From up here, the town’s circular layout is unmistakable — and so is the Attack on Titan reference. The scale and the curvature make it visceral rather than abstract.

  2. Climb the “Daniel” tower (St. George’s Church) — The 90m tower offers an unobstructed panoramic view of the circular town and, on clear days, the rim of the Ries crater in the surrounding hills. From up here you can see that you are standing inside a meteorite crater. Small climbing fee.

  3. Town center walk — The Marktplatz, half-timbered houses, and narrow alleyways look remarkably like the Survey Corps’ home district in the anime. Walk slowly.

  4. Rieskrater Museum (optional, 30–45 min) — Tells the story of the meteorite impact. NASA sent Apollo astronauts to train here before the moon landings because the crater geology resembles lunar impact sites.

Café Altreuter (Marktplatz) Traditional Swabian-Bavarian food on the main square. Try Maultaschen (Swabian stuffed pasta, the regional specialty) or a simple Brotzeit plate.

Evening back in Munich: - Hofbräuhaus (Platzl 9) — iconic, touristy, but worth once: 1-liter Maß steins, live brass band, long communal tables. Order Schweinsbraten or pretzels with Obatzda. - Or for something quieter: Weisses Bräuhaus (Im Tal 7) — Schneider Weisse brewery’s own taproom, excellent Weizenbock, authentic Munich crowd.

Day 10 Munich — Departure

Day 10 — May 30 | Munich — Departure

Morning: - Viktualienmarkt — Munich’s daily outdoor food market. The authentic Munich breakfast: Weißwurst (white veal sausage, eaten by peeling the skin) with sweet Senf (mustard) and a Weißbier. A Bavarian tradition eaten strictly before noon. - Marienplatz & Frauenkirche — Munich’s central square and Gothic twin-towered cathedral. Glockenspiel carillon performs at 11am and noon. - If your flight is afternoon: Deutsches Museum (Museumsinsel 1, opens 9am) — the world’s largest science and technology museum, or Nymphenburg Palace gardens (free entry, take Tram 17).

Last-minute shopping: - Mozart Kugeln (Salzburg’s chocolate balls, widely sold in Munich) - German mustard, local beer to take home

Transportation to Airport: - S-Bahn S8 or S1 from Munich Hauptbahnhof to Munich Airport (~40 min, €13.60) - Allow at least 2 hours before international departure
